# Neurofive ML Internship — Week 6, Final Task
## House Price Prediction: End-to-End ML Project + Streamlit Deployment

### 📌 Problem Statement
Buying or selling a house involves a lot of guesswork about "fair" pricing — sellers often over- or under-price homes relative to what comparable properties actually sell for, and buyers have no quick way to sanity-check whether an asking price is reasonable. This project builds a **house price prediction model** using structural and locational features of a home (area, bedrooms, bathrooms, amenities, furnishing status, etc.) so that anyone — a homeowner, buyer, or agent — can get an instant, data-driven price estimate instead of relying purely on intuition or a single realtor's opinion.

**Goal:** Given a house's characteristics, predict its likely market price (in the dataset's currency) as accurately as possible, and make that prediction accessible through a simple web app.

---

### 🗂️ Dataset
`housing.csv` — **545 rows × 13 columns**, no missing values, no duplicate rows. Each row is a house sale with:
- **Numeric:** `price` (target), `area`, `bedrooms`, `bathrooms`, `stories`, `parking`
- **Categorical (yes/no):** `mainroad`, `guestroom`, `basement`, `hotwaterheating`, `airconditioning`, `prefarea`
- **Categorical (multi-class):** `furnishingstatus` (`furnished` / `semi-furnished` / `unfurnished`)

**Basic statistics:** prices range from ~1.75M to ~13.3M (mean ≈ 4.77M), and area ranges from 1,650 to 16,200 (mean ≈ 5,151).

---

### ⚙️ Full Workflow

#### 1. Define the Problem
Predict `price` as a continuous target (regression) from structural/amenity features — a supervised regression problem, evaluated with MAE, RMSE, and R².

#### 2. Clean the Data
- Verified zero missing values and zero duplicate rows — the dataset required no imputation or de-duplication at the raw level.

#### 3. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
- **Price distribution:** histogram of `price` to check skew/shape.
- **Area vs. Price:** scatterplot showing a clear positive relationship — larger homes cost more, with increasing spread at higher areas.
- **Bedrooms vs. Price:** boxplot showing price generally rises with bedroom count, with diminishing/more variable returns at the high end.
- **Furnishing Status vs. Price:** boxplot showing fully furnished homes command a price premium over semi-furnished and unfurnished ones.
- **Correlation with price** (numeric features only):

| Feature | Correlation with Price |
|---------|:-----------------------:|
| area | 0.536 |
| bathrooms | 0.518 |
| stories | 0.421 |
| parking | 0.384 |
| bedrooms | 0.366 |

`area` and `bathrooms` are the strongest single numeric predictors of price, followed by `stories`, `parking`, and `bedrooms`.

*(See the histogram, scatterplot, boxplots, and correlation heatmap generated by the script.)*

#### 4. Feature Engineering
Two new features were engineered to capture relationships the raw columns don't express directly:
- **`area_per_bedroom`** = `area / bedrooms` — a proxy for how spacious each bedroom is, distinguishing a large house with few large bedrooms from one with many cramped ones.
- **`total_rooms`** = `bedrooms + bathrooms` — a simple combined "room count" signal.

#### 5. Preprocessing
A single `ColumnTransformer`, shared by both models:
- **Numeric** (`area`, `bedrooms`, `bathrooms`, `stories`, `parking`, `area_per_bedroom`, `total_rooms`): median imputation + `StandardScaler`
- **Categorical** (`mainroad`, `guestroom`, `basement`, `hotwaterheating`, `airconditioning`, `prefarea`, `furnishingstatus`): most-frequent imputation + `OneHotEncoder(drop="first", handle_unknown="ignore")`

#### 6. Train Multiple Models
80/20 train/test split (`random_state=42`, 436 training / 109 testing rows). Two pipelines trained:
- **Linear Regression**
- **Random Forest Regressor** (`n_estimators=100`, `random_state=42`)

#### 7. Evaluate

| Model | MAE | RMSE | R² Score |
|-------|:-----:|:-----:|:--------:|
| **Linear Regression** | **968,362** | **1,321,640** | **0.6544** |
| Random Forest | 1,052,633 | 1,420,945 | 0.6005 |

#### 8. Pick the Best Model
**Linear Regression wins** on every metric — lower MAE, lower RMSE, and higher R² (0.6544 vs. 0.6005). On a dataset this small (545 rows, 13 raw columns), Random Forest doesn't have enough data to learn a more nuanced non-linear structure than it can exploit, and it tends to overfit to noise in the training folds; the mostly-linear relationships (bigger area/more bathrooms → higher price) surfaced by the EDA correlations are captured just as well, or better, by a simple linear model. Linear Regression was saved as the final model.

#### 9. Deploy
The saved Linear Regression pipeline (`house_price_model.pkl`) was wrapped in a **Streamlit app** for live, interactive predictions.

### 🖥️ The Streamlit App

`app.py` loads `house_price_model.pkl` and presents a form with:
- Numeric inputs: Area, Bedrooms, Bathrooms, Stories, Parking Spaces
- Yes/No selectors: Main Road, Guest Room, Basement, Hot Water Heating, Air Conditioning, Preferred Area
- Furnishing Status selector: Furnished / Semi-furnished / Unfurnished

On clicking **🏠 Predict House Price**, the app:
1. Recomputes `area_per_bedroom` and `total_rooms` using the same formulas used during training
2. Assembles a single-row DataFrame with all 14 expected columns
3. Calls `model.predict()` (the pipeline handles scaling/encoding internally)
4. Displays the predicted price formatted as currency, plus a recap of the entered details

**Correctness check:** the app's input columns (`area`, `bedrooms`, `bathrooms`, `stories`, `mainroad`, `guestroom`, `basement`, `hotwaterheating`, `airconditioning`, `parking`, `prefarea`, `furnishingstatus`, `area_per_bedroom`, `total_rooms`) exactly match the 7 numeric + 7 categorical features the saved pipeline's `ColumnTransformer` was fit on, and the engineered-feature formulas match the training script exactly. The app is correctly wired to the model.

### ▶️ How to Run

**1. Train the model:**
```bash
python train.py
```
This loads `housing.csv`, runs EDA, trains both models, prints the comparison table, and saves the best model as `house_price_model.pkl`.

**2. Run the app locally:**
```bash
streamlit run app.py
```
Opens at `http://localhost:8501`. Enter house details and click **🏠 Predict House Price**.

**3. Deploy for free (Streamlit Community Cloud):**
1. Push `app.py`, `house_price_model.pkl`, and `requirements.txt` to a public GitHub repo.
2. Go to [streamlit.io/cloud](https://streamlit.io/cloud), sign in with GitHub, click "New app," select the repo and `app.py` as the entry point.
3. Deploy — you'll get a shareable public URL.

*(Hugging Face Spaces with the Streamlit SDK is an equally good free alternative.)*

### 📝 Case Study: Real-World Value of This Project

**The problem it solves.** Pricing a home is one of the highest-stakes, least-standardized decisions a household ever makes. Sellers commonly rely on a single real estate agent's comparative market analysis, which can vary significantly between agents and is often influenced by the agent's incentive to close a sale quickly. Buyers, meanwhile, frequently lack any independent way to judge whether an asking price is fair before making an offer — a knowledge gap that disproportionately hurts first-time buyers negotiating against experienced sellers or agents.

**Who benefits.** A tool like this has three natural audiences. *Homeowners and sellers* can get a fast, free, data-driven starting estimate before speaking to an agent — useful context when deciding whether an agent's suggested listing price seems reasonable. *Buyers* can quickly sanity-check whether a property's asking price aligns with its physical characteristics, flagging homes that may be significantly over- or under-priced relative to comparable properties. *Real estate platforms and agents* could embed a model like this as a lightweight "instant estimate" feature — similar in spirit to tools like Zillow's Zestimate — to increase user engagement and trust before a human agent gets involved.

**Business value.** For a real estate startup or property-listing platform, an instant price estimator is a proven engagement driver: it gives visitors a reason to input details and stay on the page longer, and it's a natural lead-generation mechanic ("get your free home valuation") that can be used to capture contact information for follow-up by agents. Even at 65% variance explained, the model is valuable as a *directional* tool rather than a definitive appraisal — it's explicitly positioned as a starting estimate, not a replacement for a professional appraisal, which is exactly how tools like this are used in practice today.

**Limitations and next steps.** The biggest gap is the absence of **location data** (neighborhood, city district, school zones, proximity to transit) — arguably the single strongest driver of real-world house prices, and something this dataset doesn't capture at all, which likely explains a meaningful share of the ~35% of price variance the model doesn't currently explain. Future iterations could pull in geocoded location features, recent comparable sales ("comps"), or macro factors like interest rates and local market trends to substantially improve both accuracy and real-world trustworthiness of the estimate.
